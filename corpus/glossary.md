# Glossary of Cancer Registry Terms

Abstract: A structured summary of one cancer case, taken from the medical record and recorded in standard coded form.

Behavior: A classification of how a tumor acts, such as benign, uncertain, in situ, or malignant.

Benign: An abnormal growth that does not invade nearby tissue and does not spread. Benign tumors are not cancer.

Casefinding: The process of identifying reportable cancer cases from sources such as pathology reports and admission records.

Central registry: A population-based registry that covers everyone diagnosed within a defined geographic area, used for surveillance.

Clinical stage: The stage assigned before treatment, based on physical exam, imaging, and biopsy.

Distant: A Summary Stage category meaning the cancer has spread to organs or lymph nodes far from where it started.

Grade: A measure of how abnormal cancer cells look compared with normal cells. Lower grade resembles normal tissue more closely.

Histology: The microscopic type of a tumor, in other words the kind of cell it is made of.

Hospital registry: A facility-based registry that tracks the cancer patients seen at one institution.

ICD-O: The International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, the system used to code primary site and histology.

In situ: Abnormal cells that remain confined to the layer of tissue where they began and have not invaded.

Localized: A Summary Stage category meaning the cancer is limited to the organ where it started.

Malignant: Cancerous. A malignant tumor can invade nearby tissue and spread to other parts of the body.

Metastasis: The spread of cancer from the primary site to a new, distant location.

Morphology: The ICD-O term for histology and behavior together.

Pathologic stage: The stage assigned after surgery, based on examination of the removed tissue.

Primary site: The organ or tissue where a cancer first developed.

Reference date: The date a registry begins including cases. Eligible cases diagnosed on or after this date are recorded.

Regional: A Summary Stage category meaning the cancer has spread to nearby tissue or regional lymph nodes.

Reportable: A condition that must be entered into the registry, as defined by the reportable list.

Summary Stage: A simple staging scheme using the categories in situ, localized, regional, distant, and unknown.

TNM: The American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system describing the primary tumor (T), regional lymph nodes (N), and distant metastasis (M).

Topography: The ICD-O term for the code that identifies the primary site.
