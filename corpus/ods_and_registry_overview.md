# Oncology Data Specialist and the Cancer Registry

## What is an Oncology Data Specialist (ODS)?

Oncology Data Specialist (ODS) is the professional credential for cancer registrars, awarded by the National Cancer Registrars Association (NCRA). It updated and replaced the older Certified Tumor Registrar (CTR) credential to reflect how data-focused the role has become. The current certified credential is written as ODS-C.

An ODS collects, codes, stages, and abstracts data on cancer cases from the medical record. The work supports cancer research, public health planning, and patient care. People usually reach the credential through an NCRA-accredited program and a certification exam.

## What a cancer registry is

A cancer registry is a specific type of disease registry whose major purposes are:

- To establish and maintain a cancer and central nervous system tumor incidence reporting system
- To serve as an informational resource for the investigation of cancer, its causes, and identification of optimal treatments
- To provide information to assist public health officials and agencies in the planning and evaluation of cancer prevention and control programs
- To serve as the foundation for cancer research and treatment advances

State and regional cancer registries are a primary source for unbiased population-based case control studies, the end points for cohort studies and clinical trials, and the starting point for survival analysis.

## What cancer registration is

Cancer registration is the process of continual, systematic collection of data on the occurrence and characteristics of reportable malignancies, with the purpose of helping assess and control the impact of cancer on the community.

## Who cancer registrars are

The foundation of the system is a group of meticulous, hardworking people called cancer registrars, who are trained to collect accurate, complete, detailed, and timely data on cancer patients. An ODS is a cancer registrar who holds the NCRA credential.

## Why cancer data matters

Clinical researchers use accurate cancer data to study potential causes of cancer and to compare which treatments offer the best outcome based on stage of disease. Medical administrators use it to plan clinical staffing and equipment. Departments of health use it to investigate potential cancer clusters and their causes. Federal agencies use cancer statistics to determine funding for prevention and treatment. All of these statistics are the result of data collected and provided by cancer registries.
