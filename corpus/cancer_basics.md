# Cancer Disease Basics

## What cancer is

Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow without the normal controls that keep tissue in balance. These cells can invade nearby tissue and, in many cases, spread to other parts of the body. Cancer is not a single disease but hundreds of related diseases that share this loss of control over cell growth.

## Benign versus malignant

A benign tumor is an abnormal growth that does not invade surrounding tissue and does not spread to distant sites. Benign tumors can still cause problems by pressing on nearby structures, but they are not cancer.

A malignant tumor is cancer. It can invade neighboring tissue and can spread through the blood or lymphatic system to form new tumors elsewhere.

## Primary site and metastasis

The primary site is where a cancer first develops. Metastasis is the process by which cancer cells travel from the primary site and start growing in a new location, called a secondary or metastatic site.

A key rule in cancer data is that a cancer keeps the identity of its primary site even after it spreads. Breast cancer that spreads to the lung is still breast cancer in the lung, not lung cancer, because the cells are breast cells.

## How cancers are named

Cancers are usually named for the kind of tissue they arise from.

A carcinoma starts in epithelial tissue, the tissue that lines organs and surfaces. Most common cancers, such as breast, lung, colon, and prostate cancer, are carcinomas.

A sarcoma starts in connective or supportive tissue, such as bone, muscle, fat, or cartilage.

Leukemia starts in blood-forming tissue and involves abnormal blood cells.

Lymphoma starts in the cells of the lymphatic system.

## Why cancer data matters

Accurate cancer data lets researchers study what causes cancer and which treatments work best. It helps administrators plan staffing and equipment, and it helps public health officials design and evaluate prevention and control programs. Population-based registry data is a primary source for unbiased studies of cancer incidence and survival.
